The connectome · FlyWire consortium · Nature, October 2024

What does a whole brain look like when every wire is known?

For a century neuroscience worked from samples: a few neurons stained, a few circuits traced. In 2024 a consortium led from Princeton, with hundreds of labs and volunteer proofreaders, finished the map of an entire adult brain, that of a female fruit fly. The raw material was a brain sliced into thousands of sections and imaged with electron microscopes; the segmentation was done by AI and corrected by people, synapse by synapse.

"A fly brain is smaller than a poppy seed. It still took about a decade, machine learning, and hundreds of people to read all of it."the scale of the task
Epoch 01 / Imaging

A whole brain sliced and photographed

The FAFB volume: one adult female fly brain cut into thousands of ultrathin sections and imaged with serial-section electron microscopy at nanometre resolution, published in 2018 as an open dataset.

2018
Epoch 02 / Segmentation

Machine learning draws the neurons

Convolutional networks traced every neuron through the image stack. FlyWire opened the result to the community as a proofreading platform, where people fix merges and splits the model got wrong.

2020 to 2023
Epoch 03 / Larva

The larval brain, complete

A related effort finished the connectome of a fly larva first: 3,016 neurons and about 548,000 synapses, published in 2023 as the first full brain wiring diagram of an insect at any stage.

2023
Epoch 04 / Whole adult brain

139,255 neurons, every synapse

The adult connectome and its cell-type atlas appeared together in Nature: 139,255 neurons, about 50 million synapses, 8,453 cell types, released openly for anyone to query.

2024
Signal path · from photon to wing

How a fly sees a threat and leaves

The connectome makes it possible to follow a signal across the whole brain instead of stopping at a region boundary. The route below is the anatomy the map traces for vision.

01 · retina

Photoreceptors

Each of the compound eye's units carries photoreceptor cells that turn light into membrane voltage.

02 · lamina and medulla

Optic lobe layers

The optic lobes, a large share of all neurons in the brain, sort the image by contrast, motion and direction in repeated columns.

03 · lobula plate

Motion detectors

Wide-field neurons integrate motion across the visual field; a looming shape reads as an approaching object.

04 · descending neurons

Down to the body

Descending neurons carry the decision from the brain to the ventral nerve cord, where the legs and wings are driven.

The specimen and its sources

Specimen

Drosophila melanogaster, adult female

The FAFB brain: one individual, imaged whole. The connectome describes this one fly; a second brain, a male, is being mapped to compare.

Method

Serial-section EM, AI segmentation, human proofreading

Sections at nanometre resolution, neurons traced by convolutional networks, corrections by hundreds of proofreaders including volunteers, synapses detected automatically.

Access

Open data

The connectome is public and queryable. FlyMind draws only the published counts and region layout; nothing here is measured by us.
